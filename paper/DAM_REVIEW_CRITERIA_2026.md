# DAM Review Criteria — 2026 Audit

Access date: 2026-08-26.

## Explicit official criteria

The current Elsevier Guide for Authors identifies *Discrete Applied
Mathematics* with algorithmic and applicable discrete mathematics and
applications of combinatorial mathematics. The manuscript should be an
original Contribution, use an English abstract of no more than 250 words and
one to seven English keywords, and provide editable source files and complete
references. The title page must identify all authors, affiliations and postal
addresses, with a corresponding author and e-mail. Highlights are optional on
the checked guide but, if supplied, must be a separate editable file with
three to five short bullets. The data policy requires repository deposit and a
link/citation, or an explanation of why sharing is not possible. Declarations
and any applicable generative-AI disclosure are required at submission.

Primary source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/discrete-applied-mathematics/publish/guide-for-authors

## Inferred referee standards

The manuscript should make the mathematical bridge independently readable,
state exactly which finite computations are certificates, avoid treating test
counts as proof, and provide replayable code and integrity information. These
are referee standards inferred from the journal’s recent graph-theory and
algorithmic papers, not additional official rules.

Recent examples show that DAM publishes exact graph-parameter results,
algorithmic domination work, Cartesian/grid problems, and computation-backed
structural results when the mathematical statement and proof boundary are
explicit. Examples include:

- *The domination numbers of cylindrical grid graphs* (2011),
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0096300310011318;
- *2-limited broadcast domination on grid graphs* (2023),
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2023.05.031;
- *Efficient total domination and related invariants in torus graphs* (2026),
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.03.047;
- *A unified approach for domination and packing problems in graphs* (2026),
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.06.002.

These examples support a narrow venue-fit claim: the present work belongs to
DAM’s graph-parameter and algorithmic-combinatorics scope. They do not prove
acceptance, novelty, or a particular level of significance.
